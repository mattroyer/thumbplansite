thumb 👍

Like finger, but for the Atmosphere.

thumb brings back .plan files, the plain-text status updates John Carmack used to post from id Software, on top of the AT Protocol. Your plan lives in your own account's repo (on Bluesky, Tangled, or a server you run), and anyone can read it with just your handle. No feed, no likes, no algorithm. Just a text file.

Install

curl -fsSL https://thumbplan.date/install.sh | sh

macOS, Linux and FreeBSD. Running it again updates thumb. Read the script first if you like (you should). Windows: grab a build from GitHub releases.

What it looks like

$ thumb simpson.example.com
Login: simpson.example.com                   Name: Homer Simpson
Last plan update: Fri Nov 20 07:58 2026 (PST)
Plan:
* ate the donuts in the break room (all of them)
* napped at the safety console. safety first, then nap
+ fixed the gutter Marge asked about in April
- jogging with Flanders

Next: Moe's.

* done, + done late, - not happening. That's the old Carmack shorthand, but a plan is plain text: write it however you like.

Use it

thumb <handle>Read someone's plan. No account needed.
thumb -n 10 <handle>Read their last 10 entries, as a log.
thumb loginSign in with your handle and an app password.
thumb editOpen ~/.plan in your editor. Save and quit to publish.
thumb postPublish ~/.plan as it is, after editing it however you like.
thumb serveRun a real finger server, so finger you@yourhost works.

How it works

  1. Your handle points to your account's ID, and your ID points to the server that holds your data.
  2. Each time you publish, thumb adds a date.thumbplan.plan record to your own repo. Your history is kept; the newest entry is your current plan.
  3. Reading is a public request straight to that server. There's no thumb server in the middle; this website is the only thing thumb runs.
